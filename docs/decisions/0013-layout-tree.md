# 0013 · One layout model: sizes, the group, placement; a drag pins, a release settles

**Context.** By v0.7b every visible layout bug left came from one cause: a pane's size, its position and its motion were written by different code per edge, per layout and per page. Five hand-written edge gestures, each with its own clamp, its own way of holding a side still while dragging (the pair's offset plus a timer that glides it back; the picture's held top; Vertical's held side, which also wrote the prompt box's width a second time), and a mirrored copy of the drag code for the prompt on the right. The prompt's top edge, its bottom in Vertical and every edge of a panel in Vertical didn't exist, and Queue / LoRAs / PNG Info shared one width. Planned work (every pane resizable from every side, per-page sizes, layout changes that move like tab switches, and later 3–4 panes on a wide screen) would each have added more of the same.

**Decision.** One model with three owners, plus one rule for gestures:
1. **Sizes:** one size per pane, per page, per orientation (side by side or Vertical; Left and Right are mirrors and share one). An edge writes only its own pane's size. The one edge shared by two neighbours (the divider) trades size between them. Every pane has every edge. A page says only what's inside its pane: a picture *fits* (its ratio links the axes), a list *fills*. Limits (min / max) come from one pure function; past a limit an edge moves on with resistance (a rubber band).
2. **The group:** panes stick to each other with one gap. Positions inside the group are derived from the sizes, never stored.
3. **Placement:** one function puts the group at its resting anchor, a value per layout (side by side: centred; Vertical: under the tab bar, the spare room at the bottom). The prompt's own anchor (top / middle / bottom of the picture) and its height modes (full / match) are values of the same model.
4. **A drag pins, a release settles.** While an edge is held, placement keeps the grabbed pane's opposite edge still, so the edge stays under the cursor and nothing else moves (every OS window works this way). Letting go removes the pin and the group settles to its resting anchor in one motion on the layout's clock; the rubber band's spring back is the same motion. No gesture ever writes a position.
5. **A tree inside.** The maths is a layout tree (rows and columns of N panes). Today's three layouts are three trees of two panes; more panes later is more leaves, not a rebuild.

All of it is pure functions in `layoutmath.js` (`arrange`, `limits`, `rubber`), unit-tested, and one gesture factory in `edges.js` that the edges are generated from.

**Alternatives considered.**
- *A table of edges (pane × side × layout).* It encoded the special cases instead of removing them: picture pages got four edges, panels two, the prompt three.
- *Everything centred, every edge symmetric.* It removes the held sides by making placement run live during a drag, but it links edges that shouldn't be: shrinking the prompt moved the picture. Rejected by the user.
- *A docking library (dockview, Golden Layout, FlexLayout).* It positions panes with its own CSS, sashes and timing, which would make it a second owner of position and motion (see 0012, rule 4). We take the algorithm (VS Code's grid, Blender's areas), not the library.
- *A constraint solver (Cassowary / kiwi.js, as in Apple's Auto Layout).* It's the precedent for "pin while dragging, release to the rules" (edit and stay constraints), but Kiln's layouts are a simple tree with a closed-form answer. A solver would add a dependency and make results harder to explain.

**Consequences.** The hand-made holds, the mirrored drag code and the scattered clamps are deleted rather than extended. Every edge behaves the same way in every layout and on every page, and the tracer can check "no gesture writes a position". Saved layouts move to a new format (v4) with a tested migration. The settle after an outer-edge drag is one visible motion (today's glide back, kept but owned by placement). More panes, a page per pane and drag-to-split become additions to the tree, not changes to it.

**Status (2026-11-02): accepted, built in increments** (`forge-wiring`, b4448e9 … ec08bd9 and the panel heights). Amended on the way, by the user's calls:
- **The prompt box's height is never stored.** It hugs its text unless an expand button is on (the composer pattern: iMessage, Slack, ChatGPT). Height drags are feedback or position choices: Vertical's divider is the picture's bottom edge (the box rides glued under it); side by side the box's top / bottom edges become position detents (iOS sheet detents) with L1.
- **One limit for every edge:** the room's ends (the window less its area gap). The prompt's 900 px cap and the 24 px least margin are gone. A picture page keeps the edit buttons' clearance on both sides at rest (a fixed centre line while you browse); a drag may take the prompt's side of it.
- **The picture is the pane** on a picture page: its edges drag the picture, its ratio moving the other side; the divider trades the prompt against the picture. Its area stays at least the tab bar's width; the picture may be smaller, centred in it. At rest the page is still an area the prompt hugs the picture inside — the last step of the model (ROADMAP v0.7c 3b).
- **The rubber band is small:** one area gap of stretch (20 px), a picture past its room by one part gap (12 px) at most — never over another part (160 px ran under the tab bar).
- **Outlines are part of their pane** (pseudo-elements): a fixed outline following its pane with a frame loop is always a frame late.
- **Panels have heights:** every pane has every edge; a shorter panel rests centred side by side, at the top in Vertical, and the tab bar rides on it.
